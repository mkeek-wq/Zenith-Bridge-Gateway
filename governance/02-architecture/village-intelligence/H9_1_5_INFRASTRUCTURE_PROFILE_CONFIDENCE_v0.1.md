# H9.1.5 Infrastructure Profile — Confidence v0.1

**Date:** 13 Jul 2026
**Phase:** H9 Village Intelligence Discovery
**Status:** Discovery Profile
**Profile Type:** Cross-Cutting Organizational Infrastructure
**Discovery Confidence:** High

---

# 1. Mission

The Confidence infrastructure is responsible for expressing how strongly the Village may rely on an assessment, pattern, mechanism, forecast or intelligence conclusion.

Confidence does not determine truth.

It provides a bounded and explainable indication of evidential and analytical strength.

---

# 2. Architectural Classification

Confidence is cross-cutting organizational infrastructure.

It provides common concepts, scoring conventions and governance controls while allowing individual capabilities to implement domain-specific confidence models.

Current implementations include:

* general Confidence Framework;
* Intelligence Confidence Registry;
* Experience Confidence Engine;
* Learning Confidence Engine;
* Mechanism Confidence Engine;
* Replay Confidence Engine;
* Similarity Confidence Adjustment Layer;
* Replay Confidence History.

Confidence therefore exists at both:

* shared organizational level;
* domain-specific implementation level.

---

# 3. Core Responsibilities

Current discovery identified responsibilities including:

* confidence scoring;
* confidence banding;
* confidence labeling;
* penalty application;
* component-level explanation;
* readiness assessment;
* historical confidence tracking;
* domain-specific confidence composition;
* confidence adjustment;
* governance constraints.

---

# 4. General Confidence Framework

The current general framework derives confidence from hypothesis testing.

Its inputs include:

* preliminary support;
* counter-evidence;
* unresolved questions.

Current penalties include:

* counter-evidence penalty;
* unresolved-question penalty.

Current confidence bands include:

* very strong;
* strong;
* moderate;
* weak;
* insufficient.

The framework therefore treats confidence as an adjusted assessment rather than a raw support score.

---

# 5. Governance of Confidence

The current Confidence Framework explicitly establishes that:

* confidence is provisional;
* confidence is not approval;
* confidence is not truth;
* human review is required;
* production mutation is not automatically allowed.

A sufficiently high score may create eligibility for human review.

It does not create automatic organizational approval.

---

# 6. Intelligence Confidence Registry

The Intelligence Confidence Registry combines several independent sources of confidence.

Current inputs include:

* Experience Registry;
* Principle Candidate Registry;
* Prediction Accuracy Registry;
* Experience Feedback Registry.

Current confidence components include:

* historical Experience;
* principle strength;
* prediction validation;
* feedback state.

The registry applies weighted composition when sufficient components are available.

This indicates that organizational confidence is multi-dimensional.

No single input is treated as sufficient in every case.

---

# 7. Domain-Specific Confidence

Domain capabilities may define confidence using factors appropriate to their own responsibilities.

Replay currently derives confidence from:

* analogue similarity;
* historical case depth;
* mechanism depth;
* taxonomy awareness;
* forecast-envelope availability.

This confidence is explicitly described as:

`an internal quality indicator, not a prediction accuracy claim`

Replay Confidence therefore measures the maturity and quality of the Replay basis.

It does not claim that a future outcome is correct.

# 9. Relationship to Evidence

Evidence provides the factual basis.

Confidence expresses the strength of the assessment supported by that basis.

Evidence quality, freshness, reliability, contradictions and counter-evidence may all affect confidence.

Confidence must not conceal weak or incomplete Evidence.

---

# 10. Relationship to Experience

Experience contributes confidence through repeated observations, accumulated cases and historical consistency.

Repeated Experience may strengthen confidence.

It does not independently establish causal validity.

---

# 11. Relationship to Mechanism

Mechanism Confidence evaluates the strength of a proposed causal explanation.

Counter-evidence, alignment, Experience count and validation state may affect that confidence.

High Mechanism Confidence does not remove the need for formal validation or human review.

---

# 12. Relationship to Replay

Replay Confidence evaluates the quality and maturity of the Replay foundation.

Its current drivers include:

* analogue quality;
* case-library depth;
* mechanism coverage;
* taxonomy maturity.

Replay Confidence should therefore be interpreted as confidence in the analytical foundation, not certainty about the future.

---

# 13. Confidence Is Contextual

Current discovery shows that confidence is not generated from one universal formula.

Different capabilities measure different forms of confidence.

Examples include:

* confidence in a hypothesis;
* confidence in repeated Experience;
* confidence in a mechanism;
* confidence in Replay maturity;
* confidence in prediction validation;
* confidence in organizational intelligence.

The shared infrastructure provides common discipline.

The domain engine determines the relevant components.

# 15. Architectural Observations

Current observations:

* Confidence is shared infrastructure.
* Confidence is implemented differently by domain.
* Confidence is component-based.
* Counter-evidence reduces confidence.
* Missing information may reduce or block confidence.
* Confidence bands support interpretation.
* Confidence can create review readiness.
* Confidence cannot create automatic approval.
* Domain confidence must state what it measures.
* Prediction confidence and prediction accuracy are not the same.
* Confidence remains provisional and revisable.

# 17. H9 Discovery Outcome

Confidence should be regarded as the Village's cross-cutting reliance infrastructure.

It expresses how strongly an intelligence conclusion may be used, based on domain-relevant components, Evidence quality, counter-evidence, historical learning and validation.

Confidence is responsible for communicating analytical strength and uncertainty.

It is not responsible for declaring truth, granting approval or guaranteeing prediction accuracy.
