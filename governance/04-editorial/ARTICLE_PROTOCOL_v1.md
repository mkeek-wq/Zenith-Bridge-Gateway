# ARTICLE_PROTOCOL_v1

Status: Active
Version: 1.0
Last Updated: 2026-07-04

Purpose:
Define how ZNBW public intelligence articles should be structured, written, reviewed, and improved.

---

# Core Principle

ZNBW articles should translate data into decisions, not ask users to translate numbers into meaning.

Articles should move from:

Data → Insight → Business meaning → Actions

---

# Standard Article Structure

1. Executive Summary
2. Key Takeaways
3. What Happened
4. Why It Happened
5. What the Data Shows
6. Historical Context
7. What Businesses Should Watch Next
8. Business Implications
9. Transparency Panel

---

# Editorial Rules

- Remove unnecessary self-reference such as "ZNBW assesses that" in public article prose.
- Minimise repetition of the same statistics.
- Use one decimal or less in narrative text unless precision is materially important.
- Use full precision only in transparency panels, audit layers, or source tables.
- Explain what the data means for operators, investors, planners, and SMEs.
- Distinguish structural trends from cyclical movements.
- State limitations clearly when data is preliminary, partial, or proxy-based.
- Do not publish articles with unresolved contamination, unit, source, or graph mismatches.

---

# Future Article Capabilities

Future premium or advanced articles should incorporate:

- Macro context
- Historical replay
- Forecast ranges
- Scenario analysis
- SWOT implications
- Recommended business actions
- Client-specific intelligence
